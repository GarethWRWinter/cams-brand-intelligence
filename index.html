03 — Brand Audit

What CAMS is. What makes it genuinely different.
What the brand hasn't said yet.

The product is extraordinary. The brand hasn't caught up. Understanding the gap — and the specific product truths that are going unsaid — is the foundation of everything that follows.

The Product — What CAMS Actually Does
Accident management. Not insurance. The distinction matters enormously.
CAMS handles the aftermath of non-fault cycling accidents in England and Wales. They arrange replacement bikes from the cyclist's local shop, coordinate rehabilitation (physio, medical, psychological support), and connect cyclists with specialist solicitors — without requiring an insurance claim, an excess payment, or even an existing policy.

Their website headline: "We keep you moving." That's the right idea — but it's buried in a functional product description, not leading the entire brand narrative.
The Single Most Powerful Product Truth
"You don't need to be insured to call us."
This one line is category-redefining. Every competitor (Laka, Bikmo, Yellow Jersey) requires a policy. CAMS doesn't. This truth has never been the primary hook in any piece of CAMS content. It should be the first line of the bio, the hook in every ambassador video, and the premise of the most-watched reel they ever produce.

05 — Consumer Psychology

Why the audience avoids this category.
And the one format that gets past the reflex.

Insurance and accident management sit in a category where the primary consumer response is avoidance. Understanding why is the key to unlocking what the content strategy must do instead.

The Avoidance Problem

When emotionally difficult content is presented — crashes, injuries, road incidents — the default psychological response is to look away. This isn't indifference; it's self-protection. People scroll past CAMS content not because they don't care, but because the topic activates discomfort they don't want to sit with.

Comic Relief understood this decades ago. The fundraising machine that reaches millions of people doesn't lead with the suffering — it leads with Lenny Henry or a Bake Off celebrity doing something ridiculous. The humor is the delivery vehicle. The disarmed audience is then ready to receive the serious message underneath.

The same psychology applies directly to CAMS. Nobody is sharing crash statistics on Instagram. But everyone shares a video of a pro cyclist stacking it spectacularly.

Purchase Psychology
People don't buy insurance. They buy the feeling of not being exposed.
CAMS sells reassurance, safety, and the confidence to ride freely. Those are emotional products, not rational ones. Content that sells the emotion ("never worry about what happens if") will always outperform content that sells the features ("replacement bike, no excess").
The E-Bike Opportunity
Higher-value bikes on roads ridden by less-experienced cyclists.
The e-bike market is growing fast and creating a new type of cyclist: older, more financially motivated, more risk-aware, often less experienced on the road. This is CAMS' highest-value prospective audience. They are not currently being targeted by any CAMS content.
The Point-of-Sale Moment
Bikmo proved it: 70%+ of insurance purchases happen when a new bike is bought.
CAMS already has BDM relationships with bike shops. The moment a customer buys an expensive new bike — especially an e-bike — is the highest-intent moment for CAMS awareness. A simple POS programme costs almost nothing. Bikmo built their business on this insight.

07 — The Content System

From ad hoc posts to a machine
that compounds over time.

Individual pieces of great content are luck. A system that produces great content consistently is a business asset. This is the full architecture — from data input to creator output — with AI at the scripting layer.

01
Analytics Audit
Pull every metric from existing CAMS content. Identify top performers by reach, saves, and shares. Reverse-engineer why they worked. The Jan 22 post (85 likes, Brad Wiggins like) tells us the listicle format has legs — but the hook needs fixing.
02
Format Library
Build a library of 10 proven formats with hook templates, structure guides, and CTA options. Every creator — ambassador or community — works from this library. No more blank briefs.
03
AI Script Engine
Feed winning formats + CAMS brand voice into an AI scripting system. Generate 20+ script variations per format per month. Creators receive scripts, not briefs — radically lowering the barrier to production and eliminating the "staring at a blank page" problem.
04
Creator Network
Scripts go to the full network: ambassadors (Wiggins, Clancy, Dowsett), community creators (Cushty Saddle etc.), BDMs, and Gareth's channels. Everyone follows the system. Brand consistency guaranteed across all output.
05
Measure & Iterate
Monthly analytics review. Kill underperformers. Double winners. Feed new data back into the AI engine. The system gets smarter every cycle. Year 2 output will significantly outperform Year 1 because the data compounds.

Strategic Hypotheses — Ranked by Expected Impact

H1 · Highest Priority

Humour outperforms every other format in a serious category.

No brand in cycling protection has tested humour-led content. The category avoidance psychology is precisely why humour works — it disarms the scroll-past reflex.

Supporting Evidence
Comic Relief: humour as the delivery mechanism for serious giving. Compare the Market: humour built the most recalled insurance brand in the UK. Both categories have identical avoidance psychology to CAMS.
Test
3 humour reels (crash stories, cycling fails). Benchmark against top 5 existing CAMS posts by reach and save rate.

H2 · Highest Priority

"Tips from a Pro" is CAMS' fastest repeatable win.

Brad, Ed, and Alex have extraordinary credibility. A listicle format filmed in 10 minutes delivers proven algorithm performance.

Supporting Evidence
The Alex Dowsett listicle reel on Gareth's channel showed strong early reach. The Jan 22 CAMS post (85 likes, Brad Wiggins like) proves the format attracts the right audience. The fix is the hook and cross-posting.
Test
3–5 listicle reels with one ambassador. Measure reach, saves, and follows per video vs baseline.

H3 · High Priority

The uninsured-cyclist truth is CAMS' most powerful hook.

"You don't need to be insured to call us" has never been the primary hook in any CAMS content. This one line rewrites the category.

Supporting Evidence
Every competitor requires a policy. CAMS doesn't. In a market where cyclists routinely skip insurance because they "can't afford it" — this truth removes the primary objection to engagement before it even exists.
Test
One reel. Hook: "You don't need to be insured to call us after a non-fault accident." Measure DM volume and hook retention rate.

H4

Cross-posting ambassador content is worth 10× what it currently delivers.

Brad Wiggins liked the Jan 22 post. He has 1.5M+ followers. That like was never turned into a coordinated cross-post. Coordinated posting — ambassador shares simultaneously with CAMS — multiplies reach with zero additional production cost.

Bikmo built 70%+ of their customer base at point-of-sale. CAMS already has the BDM relationships. The moment a cyclist buys an expensive new bike is the highest-intent moment for CAMS awareness — and it costs almost nothing to activate.

09 — Community Strategy

Build a community that markets for you.

A brand that owns a community has an audience that compounds. The goal isn't followers — it's belonging. CAMS needs to be the brand that cycling communities associate with being looked after, not just the brand that shows up after something goes wrong.

Pillar 1
The Crash Club
A community asset where cyclists share their crash stories — funny, stupid, and harrowing. CAMS owns the hashtag, features the best stories, and turns the audience into the content creators. Every user-generated crash story is unpaid CAMS content with authentic reach.
Pillar 2
Bike Shop Network
CAMS' BDM team already has the relationships. A POS programme — QR codes, in-store reel, staff briefing card — turns every bike shop visit into a CAMS awareness moment. The bike stays with the local shop (CAMS' partner model already guarantees this). Zero cost to deploy.
Pillar 3
Community Creator Programme
Cushty Saddle is already interested but held back by the "too serious" content tone. Give community creators the humour formats, the system, and a CTA brief — then let them do it their way. Authentic creator content reaches communities CAMS ambassadors can't.
Content Output Mix (Target State)

The Community Content Engine

The most scalable content in existence is content created by your community. When CAMS gives cyclists a format, a hashtag, and a reason to participate, the community becomes the publisher — and CAMS becomes the platform.

GCN, GMBN, and Cycling Weekly all built their audiences not by publishing more — but by creating reasons for the community to contribute. CAMS has a natural community hook that none of those channels have: everyone in the cycling community either has a crash story or is afraid of having one. That's universal content territory.

The Cushty Saddle pilot isn't just a content opportunity — it's a proof of concept for the entire community creator programme. If a creator who was actively reluctant to do CAMS content becomes willing because the humour format fits his style, the system is working.
