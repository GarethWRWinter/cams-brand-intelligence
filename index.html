Section 04 — Ambassador Programme Framework

What world-class ambassador programmes actually look like.

Based on best-practice frameworks from sports marketing leaders — SPORTFIVE, Launchmetrics, and direct observation of the highest-performing cycling brand programmes globally.

01

Tier 1 — Legacy Credibility

Retired elite athletes whose names carry automatic authority. Ed Clancy is already here. The role: credibility transfer, high-profile storytelling, crash narrative, national media access. Investment: high. Content volume: low. Impact: category-defining.

02

Content creators with an engaged, active audience in cycling. Amy Hudson is already here. The role: consistent CAMS-branded content, narrative campaigns, real-time audience engagement. Investment: mid. Content volume: high. Impact: reach and relevance.

Local riders, sportive participants, club cyclists with 1K–10K followers. The role: hyper-local authenticity, event activation, word-of-mouth. Investment: low per person. Content volume: very high. Impact: community building.

The ROI Framework

Media Impact Value (MIV) — the industry standard

Beyond follower counts

MIV assigns a monetary equivalent to each piece of ambassador content based on platform, reach, engagement, and audience quality. A single post from Ed Clancy (15K followers, 5% engagement) can generate more MIV than five posts from a 100K account with 0.3% engagement. The metric CAMS should be tracking is not followers. It's earned media value.

What the Highest-Performing Ambassador Programmes Have in Common

Content briefs, not guidelines

Tell them what to make

Vague brand guidelines produce inconsistent content. The best programmes give ambassadors specific, platform-native briefs: "This month we want a 30-second Reel about the first thing you think of when you hear the word crash." Structure creates consistency without killing authenticity.

Campaign timing

Synchronise with cycling's calendar

The highest-engagement periods for cycling content: Grand Tour season (May–Jul), sportive season (Mar–Jun), autumn racing (Sep–Oct). Ambassador content timed to these moments gets carried by existing audience interest. Off-cycle posts get ignored.

Emotional arcs, not product mentions

The crash narrative

The most effective insurance-adjacent content never leads with the product. It leads with the human story — the crash, the aftermath, the road back. The product is the resolution, not the premise. Every ambassador in the CAMS roster has a version of this story. None of them have told it yet.

Section 05 — Creative & Content Strategy

The content system CAMS doesn't have but needs.

A world-class content strategy is not a posting schedule. It's a system — with architecture, pillars, formats, and a voice — that compounds over time.

Brand Voice Architecture

Not this

"At CAMS, we're committed to helping cyclists get back on their bikes after an accident."

This

"You train in all weathers. You ride before work and after dark. You know every pothole on your commute. And then, one morning, a driver who wasn't paying attention makes a decision that changes everything. We're what happens next."

Voice principles

Athlete-first. Speak like someone who rides. Direct. No corporate hedging. Empowering. We exist so you can take more risk. Honest. Crashes happen. Be ready.

The Four Content Pillars

1

The Crash — Stories that build trust

Real crash stories, aftermath journeys, roads back. Ambassador-led. First-person. Raw. This is the content that converts — because it proves CAMS understands the experience, not just the transaction.

2

The Ride — Content that earns the follow

Performance, training, kit, routes — content that gives cyclists a reason to follow CAMS before they need CAMS. The currency of discovery. Every post here is brand awareness without saying "insurance."

3

The Rights — Content that builds authority

Know your rights. What to do in the 60 seconds after a crash. How to deal with insurers. Road safety data. This positions CAMS as the most knowledgeable voice in the room — not just a claims service.

4

The Community — Content that creates belonging

Club features, sportive partnerships, rider spotlights. The social proof that CAMS is part of the cycling world, not a brand that shows up when it goes wrong. Long-form community building.

Section 07 — The Creative Model

A question worth asking before the next creative budget is signed.

Most brands don't know what their creative operation truly costs — because the costs are distributed. Salaries sit in HR. Agency fees sit in marketing. Freelancers go through procurement. Nobody ever adds it up on one page.

An observation — not a conclusion

The people doing that work don't cycle.

That's not a criticism of any individual. It's a structural reality of how most brands hire creative teams. They look for skills: copywriting, social media, design. They don't look for the thing that makes cycling content resonate — the knowledge of what 4am looks like before an Alpine stage, what a crash actually feels like, what it means to have a season end in a single second.

The result is content that is technically competent and culturally unconvincing. The cycling audience — CAMS' audience — can feel the difference. They've been reading it for years from brands who don't actually understand them.

Which raises a question the data in this document now makes unavoidable.

Why this is different from an agency

Skin in the game

An agency invoices and moves on. A creative director with an ambassador role has their name and reputation attached to every piece of content that goes out. The incentive structure is completely different.

Sonder — or any external agency — will produce work that looks good in a credentials deck. They will not ride bikes. They will not have worked with the athletes in your current ambassador roster. They will not carry personal credibility with the audience you're trying to reach.

Gareth's 11 years at Sky was not a marketing education. It was an immersion in what happens when the best people in the world apply uncompromising standards to every controllable variable. That is the operating system this brand needs.

The three questions every creative director partnership should answer

What does CAMS stand for?

Not "accident management." That's a category. A brand stands for something emotionally true. Proposed position: CAMS is what allows cyclists to take the risk. We exist so you don't have to hold back.

Who is CAMS for?

Not all cyclists. The serious ones. The ones who ride in all weathers, who enter sportives, who race crits on weekends, who care about watts and tyres and tyre pressure. The audience CAMS' current ambassadors already reach.

Why would they choose CAMS?

Because the brand was there before the crash. Because CAMS was riding with them, talking to them, understanding them. Not because they Googled "cycling accident claim" at the worst moment of their year.
